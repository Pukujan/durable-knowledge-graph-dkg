# Research Ingestion

Load this methodology when adding source material or converting research into corpus events.

## Core ingestion contract

1. Preserve source bytes/artifacts before deriving knowledge when primary evidence is available.
2. Label reconstructed material as reconstructed; never silently promote it to verbatim evidence.
3. Keep claims, assumptions, challenges, rebuttals, contradictions, and uncertainty separately addressable.
4. Attach stable evidence/source refs to proposed events.
5. Include actor/model/vendor/harness/skill provenance on agent proposals and evaluator outputs.
6. Validate pack write boundaries, event schema, policy, and authorization before commit.
7. Commit durable events before any graph/vector projection work.
8. Treat retrieval rank, reranker score, embedding similarity, model confidence, and agent agreement as typed signals, never as truth state or calibrated probability unless independently calibrated for that exact use.
9. Agents normally propose; deterministic validation/policy gates decide whether durable knowledge-changing events are committed.
10. Preserve enough provenance to reconstruct what evidence, models, policies, tests, and evaluators were available at the time of a decision.

## Epistemic state model

Represent knowledge as claims with explicit lifecycle state rather than overwriting a single fact in place. The exact schema may specialize these names, but preserve the semantics:

- `PROPOSED` — candidate assertion generated by a person, model, importer, or rule. It has no authority merely because it exists.
- `EVIDENCED` — at least one addressable evidence item supports the claim and provenance is complete enough for verification.
- `CORROBORATED` — support includes materially independent evidence or independently derived validation; duplicated descendants of one source do not count as independent corroboration.
- `VALIDATED` — required deterministic checks, statistical checks, policy gates, or human review for the claim's risk tier have passed.
- `CURRENT_BEST` — a derived policy view selecting the best-supported currently applicable claim among competing claims. Agents must not self-promote claims directly into this state.
- `SUPERSEDED` — a later claim or policy view replaced this one for current use while history remains queryable.
- `DISPUTED` — credible unresolved contradiction exists.
- `REJECTED` — evaluation found the claim unsupported or invalid for the evaluated scope.
- `RETRACTED` — the producer or governing process withdrew a previously accepted claim.
- `QUARANTINED` — the claim or source is isolated because of poisoning, integrity, policy, privacy, or provenance concerns.

A state transition is itself provenance-bearing. Record the evaluator or policy, version, evidence set, timestamp, and reason that caused promotion, demotion, dispute, quarantine, or supersession.

## Evidence and confidence

Do not flatten heterogeneous uncertainty into one opaque confidence number when the underlying signals differ. Preserve typed evidence such as:

- statistical probability, interval, or posterior;
- source-quality assessment;
- extraction/classification reliability;
- semantic similarity or retrieval score;
- data completeness and freshness;
- contradiction strength;
- evaluator result and calibration;
- human verification status;
- experiment or benchmark outcome.

If a product or business view requires one summary score/tier, derive it from versioned policy over the underlying signals and retain the components used to derive it.

Source reputation is contextual, not globally scalar. Prefer assessments conditioned on task/domain/risk/profile/time, for example `reliability(source | task, domain, risk_tier, evidence_type, time)`. Promote evidence about source reliability, not an eternal source truth label.

## Anti-poisoning and evidence independence

Treat all newly ingested external or model-produced information as untrusted until it crosses the appropriate validation boundary.

1. Preserve source identity, content hash/artifact ref, ingestion time, event time when known, and extraction lineage.
2. Track evidence ancestry. Ten agents or documents that ultimately repeat one originating source are not ten independent confirmations.
3. Prevent epistemic laundering: a claim generated by an agent cannot later become independent evidence for itself merely because it was projected, retrieved, summarized, or repeated by another agent.
4. Separate source trust from claim truth. A historically reliable source can still produce a bad claim; a weak source can occasionally provide true evidence.
5. Detect contradictions and retain both sides until resolution policy has enough evidence to decide.
6. Rate-limit or quarantine suspicious high-volume sources and provenance breaks rather than allowing volume to manufacture apparent consensus.
7. Keep embeddings and semantic retrieval in the candidate-discovery path. Similarity may find evidence or possible entity matches; it must not directly promote truth state.
8. Record poisoning/rejection findings as durable security/evaluation events when policy allows, so the same attack pattern can be recognized later.

## Independent evaluation and adversarial rounds

When research feeds a high-risk engineering or business decision, preserve evidence from the evaluation process as first-class lineage rather than only the final verdict.

- Separate builder/proposer, critic/red-team, adjudicator, and mechanical verifier roles when the risk tier justifies it.
- Cross-vendor or cross-model agreement is useful metadata but is not automatically independent evidence; track shared source ancestry and common evaluation dependencies.
- Prefer deterministic/mechanical checks for properties that can be checked mechanically: schema, types, policy, execution, linting, invariants, tests, signatures, permissions, and bounded state transitions.
- Preserve SDD/TDD expectations, mutation results, hidden-holdout results, end-to-end execution results, and experiment/shadow/canary outcomes when they materially justify promotion.
- Evaluate evaluators where practical: track false accepts/rejects, mutation detection, calibration, domain/risk scope, oracle access, and historical performance.
- Re-run adversarial evaluation when defenses, prompts, models, policies, or evaluators materially change; a defense that passes a static attack is not proven against adaptive attacks.
- Keep hidden oracles/holdouts behind an information boundary. Ordinary traces and graph projections must not leak exact hidden cases or expected answers back into builders unless the evaluation is explicitly retired/declassified.

## Promotion and current-state policy

Promotion should be risk-based and evidence-based, not a fixed count of agreeing agents, sources, or scenarios.

A promotion policy may consider:

- task and business risk tier;
- evidence independence and source ancestry;
- statistical power/effect size and rare-failure coverage;
- mechanical verification results;
- adversarial/red-team outcomes;
- evaluator reliability;
- temporal freshness/applicability;
- shadow/canary/controlled-experiment outcomes;
- rollback/blast-radius constraints.

`CURRENT_BEST` should normally be computed by trusted deterministic policy over durable claims/evidence, not written directly by a generative agent. The durable corpus should retain the competing claims and the provenance of the policy decision so current state can be recomputed if ontology, calibration, or policy changes.

## Durable log vs projections

The durable evidence/event record is the source-of-truth substrate. Neo4j, Graphiti, vector indexes, keyword indexes, and other graph/search structures are rebuildable projections.

When possible, store or reference exact immutable/content-addressed source and evaluation artifacts, then link durable events to those artifacts by stable IDs/hashes. This allows historical replay and graph rebuild without converting a projection into authority.

Normal knowledge evolution is append-only/versioned. Privacy, legal erasure, secret exposure, or other exceptional removal paths must use explicit tombstone/shun/redaction policy rather than silently rewriting history.
